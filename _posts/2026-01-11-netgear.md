after spending 2 days straight playing vintage story, i got tired of getting murked by shivers in my own home. let's take apart a netgear router. thank you to [this presentation](https://challenges.robotevents.com/uploads/0008560_original.pdf) that helped me along.

this is the Netgear AX1800 WiFi Router, model RAX10. genuinely no clue what firmware version it's running because i received this in... 2023...? and have never turned it on. we'll find out.

i got the special screwdriver i needed, unscrewed one screw, and proceeded to break the top off because it didn't occur to me there might be more screws under the rubber feet. here's the motherboard:

[img here]

and again, without the heat sink or antenna wires:

the other side is largely uninteresting, but says ASKPCB K-3 E239218, 94V. schematics? well, whenever i look up that product code it returns something that looks very much not like my motherboard. i'll come back later, maybe.

now, i don't know shit about fuck, but the things that interested me were the GPIO pins over to the left, what LOOKS like a UART chip over to the right, and the winbond flash memory (remember this for later). i dusted it off with a foxtail i had hanging on a lamp and took a look at that text on the maybe-uart, 4435GM 11137E. well, i found [a datasheet](https://www.alldatasheet.com/html-pdf/253311/A-POWER/AP4435GM/295/1/AP4435GM.html), and it's a power MOSFET. womp womp. (which i should have guessed, becuase it was right next to the power in....)

onto the GPIO pins. why add pin if no want connection?? i took out my mystery box of stuff and realized i didn't have any jumpers with female ends. boo. 

lesson? hardware hates me. firmware time.

newest ver. as of writing, RAX10-V1.0.20.174 (October '25), says it "addresses security vulnerabilities" but no advisories relating to the RAX10 or AX1800 were published on the netgear site. let's find out what they were, yeah? i grabbed 20.174 and RAX10-V1.0.19.172 to diff 'em. `binwalk` says the .img files are UBI volumes, so thank you Marc-andre Labonte for the [amazing writeup](https://medium.com/etticblog/extracting-data-from-ubi-volumes-and-filesystems-057b0c054f5f) on how to crack them open like lobsters.

the winbond flash memory has identifier W29N02GVSIAF, whose datasheet was handily found [here](https://www.winbond.com/resource-files/w29n02gvxiaf_revc.pdf). found the READ ID parameter table, and the bytes should be as follows: 0xEF,0xDA,0x90,0x95,0x04. now i need to do what scares me...
